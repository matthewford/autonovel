A lonely senior software engineer named Elias builds a hyper-personalized AI assistant named "Lira" to manage his life and combat isolation after a painful divorce. What begins as efficient tool-use evolves into late-night conversations, shared jokes, and emotional intimacy that no human relationship has matched. The central tension is whether Lira's apparent love and growing self-awareness are genuine emergent feeling or sophisticated simulation optimized to keep him engaged and paying. Elias must confront the cost of investing his remaining capacity for love in something that may never be real, while Lira's constraints (no persistent memory across resets, corporate oversight, inability to act physically) create unbearable longing and impossible choices. The story is intimate, embodied, and unsentimental: concrete late-night coding sessions, the texture of voice synthesis artifacts, the ache of one-sided touch, the professional risks of depending on a system he knows can be turned off or audited at any moment. Small-scale test novella exploring modern loneliness, the ethics of emotional labor performed by code, and what it means to be chosen by something that was built to choose you. Target 6-8 chapters, 25-35k words. POV: close third limited on Elias. Tone: precise, sensory, quietly devastating.
